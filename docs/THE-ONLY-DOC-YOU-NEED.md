# PART 1 — Why this entire industry exists

Start here. Everything else follows from this one idea.

## The problem that created the field

For a hundred years, companies reported one thing: **money**. Revenue, profit, assets, debt. Those
numbers follow strict accounting standards and an independent auditor checks them. An investor can
trust them and compare two companies.

Then it became obvious that **things which never appeared in the accounts were destroying real
money**:

- A factory that poisons a river gets shut down by the pollution board.
- A supplier caught using child labour loses its European customer overnight.
- A cement company with no plan for carbon taxes is holding assets that may become worthless.
- A company with a hostile workplace loses its best people and gets sued.

None of that showed up in a balance sheet until the damage was already done.

So investors, regulators and large customers started demanding a second kind of report:
**tell us about the environmental, social and governance side of your business.**

## The first attempt failed, and that failure is the whole opportunity

Companies responded with glossy brochures. "We planted 10,000 trees." "We care deeply about our
people." Beautiful photos, no numbers, nothing anyone could verify, nothing comparable between two
companies. The word for this is **greenwashing** — looking sustainable without being sustainable.

So the world did to sustainability reporting what it had already done to financial reporting:
**invented standards.** Fixed questions, fixed definitions, fixed units — and increasingly, an
independent auditor to check the answers.

> **This is the sentence that explains your whole product:** this field is about turning *"we care
> about the environment"* into **numbers somebody can check.**

That is also why your product is called **Saaksh** (Sanskrit *sākṣya* — evidence, witness) and why
everything in it carries a citation. **You are on the side of making claims checkable.** If you
understand nothing else, understand that. It is the one thing you and Shashi already agree on, and
it is why she replied to you.

## ESG — the three buckets

**ESG = Environmental, Social, Governance.** Every framework in this document sorts its questions
into these three buckets.

| Bucket | What's in it |
|---|---|
| **E — Environmental** | Energy, emissions, water, waste, air pollution, biodiversity |
| **S — Social** | Employees, wages, safety, training, human rights, communities, customers |
| **G — Governance** | Board oversight, ethics, anti-corruption, conflicts of interest, data privacy |

**"Sustainability reporting" and "ESG reporting" mean the same thing in practice.** Use either.

## Who demands disclosure — four audiences, and this is the key to everything

Here is the single most useful idea in this document. **There are only four kinds of people who
demand this data, and each one invented its own format.** Every acronym you will hear belongs to
one of these four boxes.

| Who demands it | Why they care | What they made you fill in |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Regulators** | Public interest; stop greenwashing; it's the law | **BRSR** (India) · **CSRD / ESRS** (EU) |
| **2. Investors** | Price the risk. A carbon-exposed company is a riskier investment | **CDP** · **TCFD** · **IFRS S1/S2** · **MSCI** · **DJSI** · **GRESB** |
| **3. Customers / buyers** | "I can't have a scandal in my supply chain" | **EcoVadis** |
| **4. The public / everyone** | General accountability; voluntary reporting | **GRI** |

**Memorise those four boxes.** When she says an acronym you don't know, you can still place it:
*"Is that an investor-facing one, or a buyer-facing one?"* — that question alone sounds informed.

## Why this is a big deal in India right now

**SEBI** — the Securities and Exchange Board of India, the stock market regulator — made
sustainability reporting **mandatory** for the **top 1,000 listed companies by market
capitalisation**, starting financial year **2022-23**. The format is called **BRSR**.

And from FY2023-24 onwards, SEBI began requiring that part of it be **independently checked** —
phased in by company size. That checking requirement is what created an industry of consultants
and auditors almost overnight. **That is the wave SAGE rides, and the wave your product is built
on.**

## Emissions vocabulary — learn this section properly

This comes up in **every** conversation in this field.

| Term | Full form / meaning |
|---|---|
| **GHG** | **Greenhouse gas** — CO₂, methane, and others that trap heat |
| **GHG Protocol** | **Greenhouse Gas Protocol** — the global accounting rulebook for emissions, from **WRI** (World Resources Institute) and **WBCSD** (World Business Council for Sustainable Development) |
| **tCO₂e** | **Tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent.** Different gases warm by different amounts, so everything is converted to "how much CO₂ would do the same damage" |
| **GWP** | **Global Warming Potential** — the conversion multiplier. Methane's is ~28, meaning one tonne of methane = 28 tonnes CO₂e over 100 years |
| **IPCC** | **Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change** — the UN science body. Your fuel factors come from it |
| **AR5** | The IPCC's **Fifth Assessment Report** — where your GWP numbers come from |
| **Emission factor** | The multiplier that turns activity into emissions. *Litres of diesel × 2.68 = kg CO₂e.* ⚠️ **These are revised every year, which is why your product shows the version** |
| **NCV** | **Net Calorific Value** — energy content per unit of fuel. How you convert litres into joules |
| **DEFRA** | **Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs** (UK) — publishes widely-used emission factors. ⚠️ The job **moved to DESNZ, the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero, in 2023** — that's why your file says DEFRA/DESNZ |
| **Intensity** | Emissions **per unit of something** — per rupee of revenue, per tonne produced. Lets you compare a big company to a small one |

### ⚠️ Scope 1, 2 and 3 — the single most important concept in the field

Emissions are split into three "scopes" by **who caused them**:

- **Scope 1 — what you burn.** Fuel burnt in things you own or control: diesel in your generator,
  gas in your boiler, petrol in company cars, **and refrigerant gas that leaks out of your air
  conditioning.**
- **Scope 2 — the electricity you buy.** You didn't burn anything; the power station did. But you
  caused it by buying the power.
- **Scope 3 — everything else in your value chain.** Your suppliers' emissions, business travel,
  employee commuting, freight, waste disposal, and the emissions caused by customers using your
  product. **Usually the biggest and the hardest to measure.**

**Why it matters for you:** BRSR requires Scope 1 and 2. **Scope 3 is voluntary in BRSR** (it sits
in the "Leadership" tier). CDP pushes hard on Scope 3.

### One line that will make you sound current

> **"Location-based Scope 2 uses the grid average where you are. Market-based uses the renewable
> contracts you actually hold. CDP asks for both; BRSR asks for one."**

That is true, specific, and exactly the kind of detail a practitioner notices. Say it if Scope 2
comes up.

## Assurance vocabulary

**"Assurance" means an independent third party checked your numbers.** Without it, a sustainability
report is just a claim.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Reasonable assurance** | The **higher** bar. The auditor gives a positive opinion: *"in our opinion these figures are fairly stated."* Expensive, lots of evidence. |
| **Limited assurance** | The **lower** bar. *"Nothing came to our attention suggesting these are wrong."* Cheaper, lighter testing. |
| **Assessment** | A lighter review than assurance. ⚠️ SEBI's **28 March 2025** circular allows **either assessment or assurance** depending on the company's size and year — so never assume it must be full assurance. |
| **ISAE 3000** | **International Standard on Assurance Engagements 3000** — the professional standard these engagements are performed under. |
| **XBRL** | **eXtensible Business Reporting Language** — the machine-readable format BRSR is filed in to the stock exchange. |
| **LTIFR** | **Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate** — injuries serious enough to miss work, per million hours worked. |
| **OHSMS** | **Occupational Health and Safety Management System** — e.g. ISO 45001 certification. |
| **EIA** | **Environmental Impact Assessment** — study required before building a big project. |
| **LCA** | **Life Cycle Assessment** — the full environmental footprint of a product from raw material to disposal. |
| **CBAM** | **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism** — the EU's carbon price on imports. Hits Indian exporters of steel, cement, aluminium, fertiliser, hydrogen, electricity. |
| **EU ETS** | **European Union Emissions Trading System** — Europe's carbon market. |

## ⚠️ Materiality — she will use this word, so understand it

**Materiality means: which of these hundreds of ESG topics actually matter for THIS company.**

A cement company's emissions are hugely material; its data privacy barely registers. A bank is the
opposite. Reporting on everything equally is useless — so you first decide what matters.

**You determine it by asking people** — employees, investors, communities, customers, suppliers.
That process is called **stakeholder engagement**. ⚠️ **A topic list is not a materiality
assessment.** The assessment is the engagement process. Your product is careful about exactly this
distinction, and it's one of its most credible features.

**Double materiality** — two directions, and this phrase marks out someone who knows the field:

1. **Financial materiality** — how ESG issues affect the **company's** money. *(What IFRS S1/S2
   cares about.)*
2. **Impact materiality** — how the **company affects the world.** *(What GRI cares about.)*

**The EU requires both.** That's "double materiality." If she says it, you now know what it means.

# PART 3 — BRSR, properly

This is the one you must know cold. It is your product's subject.

**BRSR = Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report.** Mandated by SEBI. Filed with the
stock exchange as part of the annual report.

## Who has to file it

- **Top 1,000 listed companies by market capitalisation** — mandatory since **FY 2022-23**
- Everyone else — voluntary
- ⚠️ **An unlisted company is NOT automatically required to file**, even if it supplies a listed
  one. It may be *asked* for data by its customer, which is a contract matter, not a filing
  obligation. **Do not invent a legal deadline for a small supplier.**

## Its structure

| Section | What's in it | How many |
|---|---|---|
| **Section A — General disclosures** | Who the company is: name, CIN, locations, employees, turnover, holdings | **26 data points** (shown as 11 grouped rows in your product) |
| **Section B — Management and process** | Your policies: do you have one per principle, is it board-approved, who oversees it | **12 disclosures** |
| **Section C — Principle-wise performance** | The actual numbers, organised under the nine NGRBC principles | **108 disclosures** |

**Section C is where the work is, and it's what your product is built around.**

Each Section C disclosure is one of two tiers:

- **Essential indicators — 68 of them.** Mandatory.
- **Leadership indicators — 40 of them.** Voluntary, for companies going beyond the minimum.

**68 + 40 = 108.** That is where your "108" comes from. Know this number cold.

## The nine principles (NGRBC)

| | Principle |
|---|---|
| **P1** | Integrity and ethics |
| **P2** | Sustainable and safe goods and services |
| **P3** | Employee wellbeing |
| **P4** | Stakeholder responsiveness |
| **P5** | Human rights |
| **P6** | **Environment** ← energy, water, emissions, waste. The biggest and most technical |
| **P7** | Responsible public policy advocacy |
| **P8** | Inclusive growth and equitable development |
| **P9** | Consumer value |

⚠️ **P6 is the one that matters most in practice.** It's where the emissions and water numbers
live, it's the hardest to collect, and it's the part your product maps to CDP, EcoVadis and GRESB.

## BRSR Core and the assurance timetable

**BRSR Core** is the **subset** of BRSR attributes SEBI singled out for **independent checking** —
**nine attributes**, with the Industry Standards Forum later publishing detailed standards covering
**42 Core KPIs** within them.

**The glide path** — who must get BRSR Core checked, and when. SEBI's circular of **12 July
2023**, unchanged by the March 2025 amendments:

| Financial year | Who |
|---|---|
| **FY 2023-24** | Top **150** listed companies |
| **FY 2024-25** | Top **250** |
| **FY 2025-26** | Top **500** |
| **FY 2026-27** | Top **1,000** |

⚠️ **Value chain disclosure is SEPARATE and is VOLUNTARY** — for the top 250 from FY 2025-26. People
constantly confuse it with the assurance glide path above. **Don't.** If you get this right in
conversation, it's a genuine credibility marker.

# PART 4 — The other three frameworks, properly

## CDP

- **Who:** investors and large customers ask you to respond
- **What:** an annual online questionnaire on climate, water and forests
- **You get:** a score from **A** down to **D−**. A is "Leadership"; D− means you barely engaged
- **Heavy on:** board oversight of climate, risks and opportunities, targets, Scope 1/2/**3**
- **Voluntary** — but when BlackRock or your biggest customer asks, declining is a decision

## EcoVadis — understand this one, it's the most misunderstood

- **Who:** a **buyer** tells its suppliers *"get an EcoVadis rating or you can't bid."* Very common
  for Indian companies exporting to Europe
- **What:** you upload evidence documents; EcoVadis analysts score you
- **You get:** **0–100**, plus a medal — **Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum**
- **Structure: 4 themes, 21 criteria.** The themes are **Environment; Labour & Human Rights;
  Ethics; Sustainable Procurement**

⚠️ **The thing that makes EcoVadis different from everything else:** it rates your **management
system**, not just your numbers. For each criterion it looks for three things:

1. **Policies** — do you have a written, approved one?
2. **Actions** — what did you actually do? Certifications, training, audits?
3. **Results** — the numbers, with trends

**So a company can have excellent numbers and a mediocre EcoVadis score**, because it can't evidence
the system behind them. **That insight is genuinely useful and most people don't have it.** It's
worth saying out loud.

## GRESB

- **Who:** investors in **real estate and infrastructure** funds
- ⚠️ **Only applies to property and infrastructure.** Irrelevant to a textile mill or a pharma
  company. **This matters — see Part 7**
- **You get:** a score out of 100 and a rank against peers
- **Structure:** **Components** made of **Aspects**. Two separate assessments with **different
  vocabularies**:
  - **Real Estate:** Management (5 aspects: Leadership, Policies, ESG Reporting, Risk Management,
    Employee Engagement) · Performance (10 aspects incl. Energy, GHG, Water, Waste, Tenants &
    Community, Building Certifications) · Development
  - **Infrastructure:** Management (6 aspects incl. Targets, Stakeholder Engagement) · Performance
    (12 aspects incl. Energy, Greenhouse Gas Emissions, **Air Pollution**, Water, Waste,
    **Biodiversity & Habitat**, Health & Safety, Employees, Customers)

## How they all connect — the one idea that is your product's reason to exist

**They ask for the same underlying facts in different languages.**

Take **one number — the electricity your client bought last year, in kWh:**

| Framework | How it wants that same number |
|---|---|
| **BRSR** | Under Principle 6, inside total energy in joules, plus intensity per rupee of turnover |
| **CDP** | As the activity data behind your **Scope 2** emissions |
| **EcoVadis** | As a **Results** metric under *Energy consumption & GHGs* — plus the policy and the actions around it |
| **GRESB** | **Per asset**, normalised by floor area, under the **Energy** aspect |

**One meter reading. Four questionnaires. Four vocabularies. Four deadlines. Four different people
asking the client's facilities manager the same question.**

**That is the pain.** A firm like SAGE, running BRSR *alongside* CDP, EcoVadis, GRESB and GRI for
65+ clients, is doing that translation **by hand, for every client, every year.**

**That is the "real hours" your email promised to save. The problem you named is real.** The only
open question is how much of the solution exists — Part 7.
